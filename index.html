7 - Tank Process for powder coating,

    Tank 1: Degreasing
  • Purpose: Removes oils, grease, and shop dirt using alkaline cleaners.
  • Action: Heated solutions (often 75–80°C) saponify fats to expose the bare metal.
    Tank 2: Water Rinsing
  • Purpose: Cleans off residual degreasing chemicals and suspended soils.
  • Action: Prevents alkaline contamination of the subsequent acidic tanks.
    Tank 3: De-rusting / Descaling (Pickling)
  • Purpose: Dissolves rust, mill scale, and oxides using acidic solutions like phosphoric or hydrochloric acid.
  • Action: Creates a uniformly reactive surface for the chemical conversion layer.
    Tank 4: Water Rinsing
  • Purpose: Neutralizes the surface by washing away acid residues and iron salts.
  • Action: Water is typically maintained at a neutral pH 7 to stop any further acidic reactions.
    Tank 5: Surface Activation (Optional but Recommended)
  • Purpose: Conditions the metal to grow a finer, denser phosphate crystal structure.
  • Action: Uses titanium or fluoro-based activators to create "nucleation sites" for better bonding.
    Tank 6: Phosphating (The "Heart" of the Process)
  • Purpose: Converts the metal surface into a non-metallic, corrosion-resistant phosphate layer.
  • Action: Zinc phosphate is the industry standard for durability, creating a grey, microscopically porous layer that anchors the powder.
    Tank 7: Passivation / Final Rinse
  • Purpose: Seals the microscopic pores in the phosphate layer to maximize corrosion resistance.
  • Action: Uses chromate-free sealers or deionized water to prevent "flash rusting" before the parts move to the drying oven.
